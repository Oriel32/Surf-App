Four screens for the Israeli surf forecast app, drawn from a live run of the SurfCore engine. Every number below came out of the real pipeline against the real APIs — nothing here is placeholder. The screens were chosen to show the four states that are easy to get wrong, not the four that look best.

The Shikmona endpoint still answers HTTP 200 with a 4.09 m storm reading — from January. Rendered as live during a flat August evening it would be the worst bug the app could ship. The screen names the failure instead of hiding it, and strikes through the number it refuses to trust.

Sea state

  • גלאסי — swell with weak or offshore wind. The rare one; it gets the only saturated colour.
  • צ׳ופי — onshore past ~12 kt, surface torn.
  • פלטה — under 0.1 m. Nothing breaking.

Colour is never alone

  • Every state carries its Hebrew word or a glyph beside the colour, so nothing is lost in greyscale or to colour-blindness.
  • Sand marks values the app derived rather than received — Eilat's synthetic waves, Haifa's sheltering factor.

Direction, under RTL

  • Layout mirrors; compass glyphs do not. The arrow points where the wind is going in the real world — 79°, 179°, 130° on these three screens.
  • Numerals, units and clock ranges stay LTR inside Hebrew runs.

Rendered from a live swift run smoke against Open-Meteo (best_match, 168 h) and ISRAMAR. Heights are post-transform: shoaling, refraction, per-spot sheltering, then the 0.78 × depth breaking cap. Hourly bars on screen 01 are indicative of the shape the engine produced; every other figure is verbatim. Two vocabulary calls are still unconfirmed by a local surfer — the 1.5–2.2 m band (ראש) and the middle sea state (סביר), neither of which appears in these four screens.
