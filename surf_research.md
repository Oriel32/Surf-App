Product Characterization and Meteorological Data Classification Research for an Israeli Surfing Forecast AppIntroduction to Local Oceanography and End-User PsychologyDeveloping an advanced surf forecast app for Israeli beaches requires bridging the inherent gap between global meteorological model outputs and the subjective experience and immediate needs of the local surfer. The Mediterranean Sea, being a relatively closed basin, is characterized by a unique oceanographic dynamic that is fundamentally different from open oceans. In the Pacific or Atlantic Oceans, deep storms generate swells capable of crossing thousands of kilometers and reaching surf beaches days after the wind that created them has completely subsided, allowing for stable and long-term forecasting. In contrast, the wave system on Israeli shores relies almost entirely on local and regional synoptic weather systems, such as Cyprus or Libyan lows, which create short, volatile surf windows that are extremely sensitive to the coastal wind regime.This complexity requires the app to translate pure physical data—such as wind speed in knots, specific wave height in meters, and wave period in seconds—into a User Interface (UX) that speaks the organic language of the Israeli surfing community. The current research deeply analyzes the various meteorological variables, categorizes their level of importance in the decision-making process of water sports users (ranging from surfers to SUP paddlers and kitesurfers), and defines a strict information architecture separating preview and in-depth displays. The ultimate goal is to provide system developers with a complete characterization outline that enables the creation of a smart "Match Score" algorithm, relying on seabed transformation coefficients and real-time data integration.The Psychology of Surf Data and Local TerminologyFor the user interface to be perceived as reliable and professional by the target community, it must convert dry numerical data into visual and sensory terms commonly used in the daily discourse of surfers on Israeli beaches. Mathematical models present wave height in meters or feet, but the human brain, particularly that of a surfer, evaluates the wave's power and height relative to the anatomy of the human body standing on the surfboard. Therefore, the app's display engine is required to automatically adapt between the metric measurement and the verbal description.When the measured wave height ranges from 20 to 40 centimeters, the surfing community refers to this as "ankle to knee" (קרסול עד ברך). These are minor conditions classified as excellent and safe for beginners taking their first steps in the water, or for SUP paddlers looking for a calm, turbulence-free paddling workout. Beyond this range, when the wave rises to a height of 50 to 90 centimeters, the accepted description is "waist to chest" (מותן עד חזה). This is the golden range of Israeli surfing, representing an ideal height that attracts the critical mass of surfers with basic to intermediate experience, as it provides fun waves for skill development without posing a physical danger.When synoptic systems deepen and the wave height crosses the one-meter mark and reaches up to a meter and a half, the terminology shifts to "shoulder to head" (כתף עד ראש). These conditions change the user demographics in the water, as they require high physical fitness, an understanding of rip currents, and technical skill, making them suitable mainly for experienced surfers. At the far end of the scale, during powerful winter storms, the term "double overhead and above" (פעמיים ראש ומעלה) will appear. Under these conditions, the sea becomes extremely dangerous, accessible only to professionals, and requires dedicated equipment and extreme caution.Alongside wave height, session quality is determined by the sea surface condition, which is directly affected by the friction of the wind against the water's surface. The app needs to provide a verbal and visual indication describing the texture of the sea. The most basic state is "Flat" (פלטה), representing a completely still and quiet sea with no breaking waves, equivalent to degree 0 or 1 on the Douglas scale (0 to 0.1 meters). This is an ideal state for swimmers and distance SUP paddlers but completely useless for wave surfers. When there is a presence of swell along with a very weak wind regime or light offshore wind, the sea is defined as "Glassy" (גלאסי) or smooth sea. This state is considered a surfing utopia, as the water surface is as smooth as glass and the wave maintains a perfect hydrodynamic shape that allows for high-level performance. Marking these conditions with a bright blue color in the interface is expected to significantly increase user engagement. Conversely, when a brisk onshore wind blows, the water becomes "Choppy" (צ'ופי) or messy and wavy. The wind tears the sea surface, creates whitecaps, breaks the waves prematurely, and makes the surfing experience difficult, tiring, and less rewarding.Information Architecture: Data Prioritization and Categorization in the UITo deal with meteorological information overload and provide a precise response to different usage patterns, UX research indicates a clear need to divide the data into two layers of depth. The first layer is the preview display, intended for a quick glance of a few seconds—usually upon waking up in the morning—in order to make a binary decision of whether to drive to the beach. Agreement among them indicates a high level of confidence (around 92%), while data divergence signifies uncertainty that should be alerted to (around 45%).The Physics of Waves: The Dichotomy Between Open Sea and the BeachOne of the most common UX failures among global weather apps is presenting the open sea wave height forecast as if it were the wave the surfer will meet at the beach. Advanced meteorological models (such as WAM or Open-Meteo Marine based on ECMWF) calculate wave heights at a spatial resolution of about 9 kilometers (0.083 degrees) and at a distance of 10 to 25 kilometers from the shore, in areas where the sea depth exceeds 50 meters. The problem lies in the fact that the wave undergoes a complex physical journey that completely alters its structure and height on its way to the coastline. For the app to present a true, local forecast for each "spot" (surfing location), the app's engine must calculate four main hydrodynamic processes behind the scenes.First, the Shoaling process occurs. As the water oscillation approaches the shallow continental shelf, significant friction is created between the wave's base and the seabed. This friction slows down the front part of the wave, while the back part, still in deeper water, continues to move at the same speed. Consequently, the wave shortens, its energy is compressed, and it rises upwards. Second, Refraction occurs, where the wave front bends and changes its angle of advancement in an effort to arrive parallel to the shallow coastline. The third process is Breaking; the wave reaches a collapse point when the ratio between its height and the water depth crosses the physical coefficient of 0.78, at which point the water surface collapses forward, creating the surfing potential.The fourth and most important parameter for developing the algorithm is Coastline Geometry. Every beach spot in Israel has a different character in terms of exposure, natural bays, piers, or breakwaters, which block a significant percentage of the original wave energy. For the app to be accurate, it must multiply the open sea data by specific geographical transformation coefficients. Completely open beaches exposed to swell, such as Palmahim or HaTzuk beach, will receive a coefficient of about 90% of the open wave. Typical urban beaches like Tel Aviv and Netanya will receive a coefficient of about 85%. In contrast, beaches protected by artificial structures or historical ruins, such as Caesarea (where ancient Roman piers break the swell) will absorb only about 70% of the wave. The Ashdod port and its breakwaters give nearby beaches a coefficient of about 72%, while the Haifa Bay, being closed and protected from almost every direction, will receive only about 50% of the open sea wave.An edge case requiring separate programmatic attention is the Gulf of Eilat (Red Sea). Because it is a closed and narrow basin, global wave forecast models fail to map it properly. The app must present synthetic data in Eilat, calculated directly from the local wind speed, as this area experiences only wind waves (Wind Chop) with no swell at all. The recommended formula for calculating wave height in Eilat is multiplying the wind speed in knots by 0.04, and calculating the wave period as 3 plus 0.15 times the wind speed.Wind Dynamics and Their Impact on Water SportsThe wind is the engine of the sea, but its significance varies dramatically among different sports communities. The system must allow customization according to the user's chosen sport. Wind strength in marine systems is always measured in knots, where each knot is equivalent to 1.8 kilometers per hour (km/h). For example, a 20-knot wind translates to a speed of 36 km/h.Wind considered weak ranges from 0 to 10 knots. These conditions are the aspiration of surfers, SUP paddlers, and bathers, as the sea remains stable and orderly. Moderate wind ranges from 10 to 15 knots. At this intensity, the sea surface condition begins to deteriorate for surfers, but it becomes ideal for training and instructing beginner windsurfers using wide, high-volume boards (160-225 liters) and large sails adapted for these conditions. When the wind strengthens and crosses the 15-knot threshold, it is defined as strong wind. Under these conditions, surfing beaches empty of regular surfers and fill with kitesurfers, advanced windsurfers, and wing foilers, whose ideal working range is usually defined between 12 and 22 knots.The direction from which the wind blows has a crucial impact on the nature of the surf and safety at sea.
When a westerly wind (Onshore) blows, it comes from the open sea and pushes the water towards the shore. This wind increases wave height but simultaneously destroys their structure, causing the sea to be messy, choppy, and making paddling out extremely difficult. Most summer days in Israel are characterized by a westerly sea breeze in the afternoon.
A side wind, whether northerly or southerly (Side-shore), blows parallel to the coastline. When such a wind is weak (especially northerly), it leaves the sea comfortable and smooth. When it strengthens, it creates perfect conditions for kite and windsurfers, as it allows fast and stable cruising perpendicular to the wind, from the shore to the deep and back.Drift Dangers and Safety Alerts in the Interface (Easterly Wind)The easterly wind (Offshore) blows from the land out to the open sea, and typically characterizes the transitional seasons and early morning hours of the chilly winter. For advanced surfers, this wind is a gift; it caresses the water's surface, creates a "glassy" sea, delays the wave's breaking (prevents premature collapse), and helps form the most perfect and clean "barrel" shape.However, a brisk easterly wind poses a real life-threatening danger to bathers, beginners, and SUP/kayak paddlers. The danger stems from an "optical illusion"—looking from the coastline, the sea appears completely flat and incredibly calm, tempting beginners to enter the water. Yet, the moment the surfer or paddler moves a few dozen meters away from the shore, beyond the wind shadow of buildings and cliffs, the wind hits with force and pushes them out to sea, often without any ability to paddle back against the wind's power. Therefore, responsible UX characterization requires implementing an alerts mechanism that will pop up prominently in the event of an easterly wind exceeding a threshold of about 10 knots, explicitly warning against entering the water on a floating vessel for beginners and warning of the danger of drifting out to sea.Model Verification via Real-Time Observational OceanographySurfers often experience bitter disappointment when they wake up to a sea that was supposed to be full of waves according to weather models, but in reality, they find a flat sea. These gaps stem from the fact that forecasts, even from reliable models like GFS, are updated twice a day (00Z and 12Z cycles) and can miss micro-climatic changes. The only way to build long-term trust in the product is to integrate an objective, external real-time verification layer.The most important information source in Israel in this context is the Israel Oceanographic and Limnological Research (ISRAMAR), which operates the National Oceanographic Data Center (IODE). This institute operates a network of monitoring stations and marine buoys along the coastline that produce open Near Real-Time data via APIs. Connecting the app to the main buoys—the Shikmona buoy (Haifa), the Hadera station, and the Ashkelon/Ashdod station—will allow pulling critical real-time data such as significant wave height (reflecting the average of the highest third of the waves), peak wave period measurement, water temperature, currents data, and sea-state. Displaying a current measurement, for example indicating that the wave height at the Shikmona buoy stands at 4.09 meters with a period of 11.10 seconds at 21:00, absolutely verifies the arrival of a storm and eliminates forecast guesswork.Another essential verification tool is integrating video from community and commercial webcams deployed at strategic points (Herzliya, Tel Aviv, the North, etc.). The surfer's ability to see with their own eyes the breaking frequency and surfer density at the starting point contributes crucially to the overall user experience.Multidisciplinary "Match Score" Engine DevelopmentThe highest added value of an advanced app is its ability to save the user from having to mentally calculate the combined meaning of wind, swell, period, and their directions. Through algorithmic development, ideal condition ranges can be defined for each sport, weighting them into a final score ranging from 0 to 100, reflecting their proximity to utopian conditions.When building the weights, the algorithm must account for conflicting elements. For surfing, an adjusted shore wave height of 0.6 to 1.5 meters will grant full points for the height variable; however, if the period is lower than 5 seconds (indicating weak wind waves), the overall score must drop significantly. As the period approaches 7 to 9 seconds, the energy is greater and the score will rise. Additionally, a light easterly wind will provide a point bonus, whereas a westerly wind above 12 knots will deduct dozens of points due to wave structure destruction. Conversely, if the user selects a "Kitesurfing" profile, the logic flips: the wind variable becomes the heaviest weight. A score of 100 will only be given if there is a stable side or south-westerly wind in the range of 15 to 22 knots.Summary and Systemic ConclusionsThis comprehensive research proves that building a surfing forecast app for the Israeli audience cannot rely solely on pulling basic API data from a global model and presenting it in a table. The User Interface (UX) and the behind-the-scenes logic must be actively adapted to the characteristics of the Mediterranean Sea and the surfers' language, as reflected in local terminology for wave heights and water textures.The main challenges in realizing the project involve developing a software layer that performs transformation calculations (Shoaling, Refraction, Breaking), adjusting the forecast to the specific geometry of each and every beach in Israel, to resolve the gap between what is predicted in the open sea and what actually breaks on the coastline. To this, closed basin constraints like the Gulf of Eilat must be added, requiring synthetic formulas.
Proper information prioritization—where the base display answers 'yes and no' questions and incorporates match scores for the chosen sport alongside critical safety warnings (drift in easterly wind), while the expanded view allows for in-depth analysis of swell period and observations from buoys and cameras—will form the key points for the product's success, reliability, and organic growth among the local marine community. Working based on this characterization outline will ensure the development of an app that accurately reflects the field experience and empowers surfers to make informed decisions in real time.
