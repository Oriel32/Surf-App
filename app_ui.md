Product

TripPilot, an iOS travel-planning app for people who want a trip shaped for them rather than a list of links. Audience skews 25–40, design-literate, books two or three trips a year. The welcome screen has one job: make the destination feel reachable before asking for a login. The home screen has one job: get the person into a conversation about their next trip in a single tap.

Art direction

Vibe words: lagoon, sunlit, weightless, unhurried, high-clarity. The interface should feel like it was photographed at 10am in the tropics — not like a dark-mode dashboard with a beach photo pasted in. Color saturation lives in two places only: the aqua canvas and the photography. Everything structural is white, near-white, or ink.

Take one risk and spend it on the aqua: on the welcome screen it is the entire canvas, not an accent bar. Do not hedge it with a dark scrim.

Typography

One family throughout: a geometric sans with slightly rounded terminals and a true single-storey a in the display weights. Use Poppins or Outfit for an exact match to the reference; Satoshi or General Sans if you want the same geometry with less familiarity. Weights 300, 400, 500, 700 only.

The mixed-weight headline is a signature, not a flourish — the light line states the promise, the bold line states the payoff. Keep that relationship on every screen that gets a headline.

Shape and elevation

Device frame 48px radius. Cards 28px. Photographs inset in cards 22px. Buttons full pill at 56px tall. Floating glass bar 20px. Icon chips 14px, avatars and utility buttons perfect circles at 36–44px.

No visible borders anywhere except a 1px 
#F2F6F8 hairline where two white surfaces stack. Shadows are wide and nearly invisible: 0 12px 32px rgba(16,44,52,0.08) for cards, 0 2px 8px rgba(16,44,52,0.05) for buttons and the tab bar. Never a dark or tight shadow.
